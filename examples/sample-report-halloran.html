03

Obligations, before anything about maturity

These are not scores and they are not our opinion. Each is something in force today that we found is not in place. They sit above the rest of this document because they are not aspirations.

UK GDPR Art 22C — in force 5 Feb 2026 (Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 s.80)

For any significant decision made without meaningful human involvement: are the four safeguards documented — inform, representations, human intervention, contest — no.

Article 22 was replaced in February. The regime moved from prohibition-with-exceptions to permitted-with-documented-safeguards, so the burden is now evidencing the four. Test whether the human reviewer can actually overturn — the ICO's March 2026 recruitment report found rubber-stamping presented as oversight.

UK GDPR — in force

Is there a current DPIA for each AI use touching personal data — no.

Unchanged by the reforms, and the ICO can compel production of them.

We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice. It is a screen against what is currently in force, run so that these are on the table rather than discovered later. Anything deferred by the Digital Omnibus to December 2027 or August 2028 is deliberately excluded — a readiness report that scores you against obligations that do not yet apply is one your own risk function will pull apart.

06

How to read the scores

Every dimension is scored from 1 to 5 on the same scale. The stages describe how deliberate and repeatable a practice is, not how much technology is in use.

1 · Exploringcurious, nothing running
2 · Experimentingpilots, no repeatable path
3 · Operationalisingfirst things running for real
4 · Scalingrepeatable, spreading, measured
5 · AI-nativehow the business works

Four dimensions carry half as much weight again as the others — data, the reach of AI into your own knowledge, the quality of your internal platforms, and how strongly your work is driven by real users. These are the ones the research finds everything else depends on. The weights are shown against each dimension.

How we arrived at each number. Every dimension was scored twice, by different means: our assessment against a written description of each stage, and a checklist of things that either exist in your organisation or do not — a published policy, named data owners, prompts under version control, a log of what your automations have done. Where the two disagreed we have taken the midpoint and said so in the text, rather than reporting the more comfortable of the two.
