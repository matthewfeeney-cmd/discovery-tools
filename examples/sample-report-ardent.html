02

Three things to deal with now

Most of this report is about maturity — where you are and how to move. The following are different. They are things already happening in your organisation that carry exposure today, so they sit outside the plan and should not wait for it.

Before anything else on the roadmap

AI governance, risk & assurance

Name who owns AI risk today, and state in writing what may not go into an AI tool until the controls exist. This is a holding position, not the policy.

Why now. An FCA-regulated firm cannot evidence how an AI-assisted customer decision was reached. On the personal-lines book that is a Consumer Duty exposure, not a maturity gap.

Data ecosystem health

Stop any AI work that depends on the unreconciled data until the first use case has a golden record. Reconcile one domain, not all of them.

Why now. Every AI use case will pay the integration cost again, and the first one will be quoted as if it were the last.

Agentic readiness

Inventory every agent already running and put it on scoped, non-personal credentials or switch it off. Do this in week one, before anything else on this list.

Why now. Nobody can reconstruct what has been sent to policyholders, or on whose authority. For an FCA-regulated firm that is an evidencing failure with a live customer population.

Each of these should be closed, or consciously accepted in writing by a named executive with a date against it. Either is a decision. Leaving them open is not, and it is the state we found them in.

03

How to read the scores

Every dimension is scored from 1 to 5 on the same scale. The stages describe how deliberate and repeatable a practice is, not how much technology is in use.

1 · Exploringcurious, nothing running
2 · Experimentingpilots, no repeatable path
3 · Operationalisingfirst things running for real
4 · Scalingrepeatable, spreading, measured
5 · AI-nativehow the business works

Four dimensions carry half as much weight again as the others — data, the reach of AI into your own knowledge, the quality of your internal platforms, and how strongly your work is driven by real users. These are the ones the research finds everything else depends on. The weights are shown against each dimension.

How we arrived at each number. Every dimension was scored twice, by different means: our assessment against a written description of each stage, and a checklist of things that either exist in your organisation or do not — a published policy, named data owners, prompts under version control, a log of what your automations have done. Where the two disagreed we have taken the midpoint and said so in the text, rather than reporting the more comfortable of the two.

How we assessed this

So that you can weigh the findings, and challenge them.

  • 10 interviews across leaders & sponsors, technical & engineering, practitioners, alongside direct observation of how work is done.
  • 28 of 28 questions answered across 14 dimensions, of which 45% were confirmed against something we could see rather than taken on description alone.
  • Nothing here is self-reported. No survey was circulated for your teams to score themselves.

What this assessment does not cover. It examines your organisation's readiness. It does not price individual use cases, model return on a specific investment, or assess any workflow in depth, and no code was audited. We would rather say so than imply otherwise.

Where the framework comes from. Seven of the 14 dimensions and their relative weights are adapted from published 2025 research into the capabilities that predict whether AI investment produces results; that research measures amplification rather than maturity, so the stages, the ceiling rule and the sequencing are ours. The governance dimension is additionally anchored to the ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Annex A control objectives and the NIST AI RMF core functions, so that what we found there can be carried into a certification or assurance programme rather than sitting beside one. That anchoring is at objective and function level; it is not a control-level Statement of Applicability and does not constitute an audit.
