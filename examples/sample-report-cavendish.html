03

How to read the scores

Every dimension is scored from 1 to 5 on the same scale. The stages describe how deliberate and repeatable a practice is, not how much technology is in use.

1 · Exploringcurious, nothing running
2 · Experimentingpilots, no repeatable path
3 · Operationalisingfirst things running for real
4 · Scalingrepeatable, spreading, measured
5 · AI-nativehow the business works

Four dimensions carry half as much weight again as the others — data, the reach of AI into your own knowledge, the quality of your internal platforms, and how strongly your work is driven by real users. These are the ones the research finds everything else depends on. The weights are shown against each dimension.

How we arrived at each number. Every dimension was scored twice, by different means: our assessment against a written description of each stage, and a checklist of things that either exist in your organisation or do not — a published policy, named data owners, prompts under version control, a log of what your automations have done. Where the two disagreed we have taken the midpoint and said so in the text, rather than reporting the more comfortable of the two.

At most maturity levels the readiness picture picks the starting point for you; at yours it does not, which means the choice is a commercial one rather than a technical one. Pick on value, on who will sponsor it, and on which one you can measure — and that is what the next piece of work is for.

What the next engagement does with these: takes one workflow end to end, sizes three or four candidate use cases against your own numbers, and returns a costed case with a go, adjust or stop against each. At your level the gating is no longer the interesting part; the numbers are. Expect that work to be about value, effort and payback rather than about whether the foundations will hold.

How we assessed this

So that you can weigh the findings, and challenge them.

  • 2 interviews across leaders & sponsors, technical & engineering, alongside direct observation of how work is done.
  • 34 of 34 questions answered across 17 dimensions, of which 6% were confirmed against something we could see rather than taken on description alone.
  • Nothing here is self-reported. No survey was circulated for your teams to score themselves.

What this assessment does not cover. It examines your organisation's readiness. It does not price individual use cases, model return on a specific investment, or assess any workflow in depth, and no code was audited. We would rather say so than imply otherwise.

Where the framework comes from. Seven of the 17 dimensions and their relative weights are adapted from published 2025 research into the capabilities that predict whether AI investment produces results; that research measures amplification rather than maturity, so the stages, the ceiling rule and the sequencing are ours. The governance dimension is additionally anchored to the ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Annex A control objectives and the NIST AI RMF core functions, so that what we found there can be carried into a certification or assurance programme rather than sitting beside one. That anchoring is at objective and function level; it is not a control-level Statement of Applicability and does not constitute an audit.
