The census FIRST

Six questions, before you score anything. They decide which dimensions apply at all — and that matters more than it sounds: assess a business with no engineering function against paved roads and version control and you will report a ceiling it does not have.

Regulatory screen FLAGS, NOT SCORES

Binary, about fifteen minutes. None of this is maturity — it is either true or it is not, so it produces flags rather than a score. Only what is actually in force today; anything the Digital Omnibus deferred to December 2027 or August 2028 is deliberately excluded.

The frame DAY ONE

Half a day with the sponsor, before you score anything. None of it is scored. Its whole job is to keep the roadmap short and real — without it, every dimension below target generates a chain of actions and the client receives a backlog instead of a plan.

"Cut cost-to-serve" is an outcome. "Quote a renewal in an hour instead of a day" is a capability, and it's the one that tells you which dimensions matter. If they only give you outcomes, ask what would have to be true.

Ask for the thing that stalled, not the thing that worked. A roadmap that recommends what they already failed to do, without naming why it failed, will fail the same way.

What they triedWhenHow it endedWhy

Data, platform, or ways of working. The roadmap should dock onto existing programmes rather than duplicate them — or name one as the reason a dependency won't land.

ProgrammeOwnerWhat it coversWhen

Named people and days per month. "The IT team" is not an answer. This is what turns a list of gaps into a plan someone can commit to, and it's the question most likely to halve the roadmap while you're still in the room.

NameRoleDays / month

Where their three and ours agree, you have a mandate. Where they differ, that difference is the most useful slide in the readout — and their three will happen whether or not they're on our list.

1Team readiness & skills

The organisation for a small business; the department or team for a large one. Scored once, ~30 minutes with the sponsor, IT and a team lead. This module stands alone — it's a deliverable in its own right. Readiness is a weakest-link system: the lowest dimension caps everything above it.

Interview log — who told us what

Over a two-week scan you'll speak to 12–20 people. Log each one: it's the audit trail behind every score, and the source list in the report.

Team skill scan — can these people work with AI?

Seven capabilities, scored across the team as a whole: the typical level people are at, and how widely it's held. Levels: 0 none · 1 aware · 2 with help · 3 independent · 4 can teach. Required levels rise with the 12-month target you set — so a Stage 4 ambition demands more than a Stage 2 one.

Cohort breakdown — who is short of what

A team-wide average hides the shape. Score each capability for the three cohorts separately — that's what turns a skills profile into a training plan someone can cost. Leave a cell blank where the capability isn't relevant to that cohort.

Codebase review — Overwatch engine, Ground Truth scoring

Run where the client has their own product or in-house development. One scan, two report angles: engineering quality and AI readiness of the codebase. The FDE owns it across both weeks. Skip it for a non-technical department — Technology is then scored by interview alone, and the report says so.
